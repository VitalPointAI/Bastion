**Gap analysis:**
Eigenvector centrality measures recursive influence: a node's score is proportional to the
sum of the scores of its neighbors. This captures "hidden influencers" — actors with few but
strategically important connections — that degree centrality misses entirely.

The current PageRank is not iterative and therefore equivalent to degree centrality. True
PageRank adds a damping factor (typically 0.85) and random teleportation, making it a
variant of eigenvector centrality on a modified adjacency matrix.

**Implementation constraint:**
Neo4j GDS (Graph Data Science) plugin is NOT installed. All algorithms must be implemented
as either Cypher approximations or Node.js server-side computation using adjacency data
fetched from Neo4j.

**Approach: Power Iteration in Node.js**
Both eigenvector centrality and PageRank can be computed via power iteration:
1. Fetch adjacency list from Neo4j in a single Cypher query
2. Initialize uniform score vector
3. Iterate: multiply by adjacency matrix (or modified matrix for PageRank), normalize
4. Converge when max score delta < epsilon (typically 20-50 iterations)
5. Return ranked results

This avoids GDS dependency while producing accurate results for graphs up to ~10k nodes.

<notes>
**Why eigenvector centrality matters for RAFT analysis:**

In a RAFT graph, actors are connected by relationships with strength weights. Degree centrality
tells you "who has the most connections" — but in geopolitics, the number of relationships is
less important than *who* you're connected to. A small nation with defense pacts to two
superpowers may be more strategically pivotal than a nation with many bilateral agreements to
minor states.

Eigenvector centrality captures this by propagating influence recursively: your score depends
on the scores of your neighbors. This naturally surfaces:

1. **Hidden power brokers** — Few connections, but all to top-tier actors
2. **Structural bridges** — Actors connecting influential but separate clusters
3. **Vulnerability nodes** — Removing them would disproportionately fragment influence networks

When eigenvector and PageRank rankings diverge for an actor, that actor has a structurally
interesting position worth analyst attention. The centrality comparison endpoint automates
this divergence detection.

**Performance note:**
Power iteration is O(iterations * edges). For RAFT graphs (typically <1000 actors, <5000 edges),
convergence in <50 iterations means sub-second computation. No performance concern at expected scale.
</notes>
