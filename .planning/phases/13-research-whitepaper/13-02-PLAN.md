**Section Structure:**

## 2.1 Blockchain Fundamentals

**What is a blockchain?** (~3-4 paragraphs)
- Distributed ledger technology - data replicated across network
- Immutability - once recorded, cannot be altered without detection
- Transparency - all transactions visible to participants
- Consensus mechanisms - how network agrees on state (mention proof-of-stake)
- Define key terms: blocks, transactions, cryptographic hashing
- Avoid: cryptocurrency speculation, token economics, mining

**Why blockchain matters for coordination** (~2-3 paragraphs)
- Trust without central authority
- Auditable history of decisions
- Tamper-resistant records
- Multi-party coordination without single point of failure

## 2.2 Decentralized Autonomous Organizations (DAOs)

**What is a DAO?** (~3-4 paragraphs)
- Organization governed by rules encoded in smart contracts
- Members vote on proposals; smart contracts execute decisions
- No central authority - governance distributed among participants
- Transparent decision-making with immutable record
- Examples: governance DAOs, treasury DAOs, protocol DAOs

**Smart Contracts** (~2-3 paragraphs)
- Programs that execute automatically when conditions are met
- Encode rules, policies, and constraints
- Cannot be altered after deployment (immutable enforcement)
- Conceptual explanation only - no code examples per CONTEXT.md

**DAO Governance Mechanisms** (~2-3 paragraphs)
- Proposal submission and voting
- Quorum requirements and thresholds
- Time-locked execution
- Multi-signature requirements for critical decisions
- Human-in-the-loop vs autonomous execution

## 2.3 Web3 Principles

**User-Owned Data** (~2 paragraphs)
- Data controlled by users, not platforms
- Cryptographic keys for identity and access
- Interoperability across systems

**Decentralization Benefits** (~2 paragraphs)
- Resilience - no single point of failure
- Censorship resistance
- Reduced trust requirements
